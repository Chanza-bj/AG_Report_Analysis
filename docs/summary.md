# Audit findings in Zambia's Auditor-General reports, financial years 2021 to 2025

*Prepared for policy analysts, journalists, and research institutions. Figures are transcribed from the source reports with page citations in the accompanying datasets. Audit findings record matters the Office of the Auditor General could not resolve by its cut-off date. They are not proof of wrongdoing, and the reports state that explanations from Controlling Officers may still be pending.*

## Key findings

The reported value of audit findings can be compared only for financial years 2021, 2022, and 2023, because a standardised "Trend Analysis of Audit Findings" table appears only in the 2022 and 2023 reports. On that basis the total fell each year: K133.25 billion (2021), K103.52 billion (2022), and K81.46 billion (2023).

A single line, "Failure to Collect Tax Arrears", drives the total and its movement. That figure is sourced by the Office from the Zambia Revenue Authority's own annual report, and it measures a stock of uncollected tax arrears rather than an in-year loss. It was K85.78 billion, K97.86 billion, and K66.99 billion across the three years. With this line removed, the remaining findings total K47.47 billion (2021), K5.66 billion (2022), and K14.47 billion (2023). The high 2021 figure rests mainly on two large categories reported that year, "Over Commitment" (K16.86 billion) and "Uncompetitive Procurement" (K12.83 billion), each of which falls to near zero the next year. The reported value therefore does not trend smoothly; it is concentrated in a few categories.

The 2024 and 2025 reports do not publish a comparable total. They present findings as a narrative list of notable observations, so a category value for those two years is recorded as NA.

The same portfolios recur in every report. Across the five years, nine portfolios appear in all five: Office of the President (Provincial Administration), and the Ministries of Finance and National Planning, Agriculture, Home Affairs and Internal Security, Education, Health, Fisheries and Livestock, Community Development and Social Services, and Lands and Natural Resources. Measured by the number of unresolved-findings paragraphs over 2021 to 2025, provincial administration under the Office of the President has the most (43), followed by Finance (18), Agriculture (17), Home Affairs (15), Education (12), and Health (11). The count of findings paragraphs per report declined overall: 52, 40, 28, 32, and 27.

## Methods note

The measure for value is the Office's own "query category" amount in Kwacha. The measure for recurrence is the count of Table-of-Contents findings paragraphs, attributed to a portfolio by the printed entity name (Head codes are reused for different ministries between years, so names, not codes, identify a ministry). Each summary table page and Table of Contents was rendered from the PDF and read directly, not taken from raw text extraction, which misaligned the table columns. Three internal checks passed: the 2022 column total is identical in the 2022 and 2023 reports (K103,519,546,579); the two reports agree category by category except for a K7.26 million reallocation between two farming-input categories that nets to zero; and printed variances match recomputed variances in 75 of 76 rows, the exception being a one-Kwacha rounding difference. The printed annual totals for 2021 and 2023 each differ from the sum of their components by one Kwacha.

## Limitations

The category value series covers only 2021 to 2023. The 2024 and 2025 reports changed format and report no comparable total. Totals are dominated by one externally sourced tax-arrears figure. Category labels and the set of categories change between years, and some contain transcription errors in the source (for example "Tranfer", "Withdraw"). US dollar amounts in the narrative are not converted and are excluded from Kwacha totals. Paragraph counts measure the volume of findings, not their monetary size. The analysis covers the published main reports only; it does not include underlying working papers or later Treasury Minute responses.
