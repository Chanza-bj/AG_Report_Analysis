The problem

Zambia's Auditor General publishes a Report on the Accounts of the Republic each year. The five reports for 2021 to 2025 run to 1,862 PDF pages. They are the best public record of how government money was spent and where it went wrong, but almost nobody reads them. The layout changes between years, some tables exist in one year and not the next, and the same supplier or ministry can be named in several different ways.

I set out to turn them into a structured dataset where every contract and supplier row carries the PDF page it came from, so that a journalist, a committee clerk or a citizen can check any claim in under a minute.

What I built

LayerRowsHow it was built

Alongside these: budget execution and surplus for 2022 to 2025, findings by category, repeat findings, audit opinions and stated ministry underfunding. Fourteen R scripts rebuild and check the dataset from the extracted report text and the source CSVs. A dashboard, a one-page brief and a written report sit on top.

How I checked it

Sampling. I drew a random sample (fixed seed, so anyone can rerun it) from the three bulk layers and re-opened each row's cited page.

30 / 30contract amounts on their cited page
25 / 25organisation names on a cited page
19 / 20cases; the miss was a placeholder label, not a wrong page

Coverage, not just accuracy. Sampling only tells you whether the rows you have are right. It cannot tell you what is missing. When I looked for agencies I knew appeared in the reports, the organisation index was missing ZAMMSA, NAPSA, Trade Kings and Zambia Seed Company. I measured coverage against every company name in the text and found it at 73%. The cause was an extraction step that filtered out acronym names, plus a bug that discarded names split across the PDF's column spacing. After rebuilding the extractor, the index grew from 594 to 1,231 names, and coverage rose to 90% matched directly and 94% once extraction artifacts are set aside.

Errors I found and corrected, each recorded as a new version in the changelog:

  • Overstated wording. The dashboard said K786 million was "paid for nil delivery" to 13 seed suppliers. The report says they had not delivered by the 15 January deadline, and a July 2023 verification found most delivered by March. It now says "paid before delivery".
  • Hand-typed counts that drifted. A supplier total read 471 against 479 computed, and a caption said 32 contracts when there were 34. Every count now generates from the data.
  • A correction that was itself wrong. I cut the 2021 bar from the findings chart on the belief that no comparable total existed for that year, and later thought the category data totalled K237 billion instead of K103.5 billion. Both came from reading the report year as the financial year: each report prints a two-year comparison table. Reconciling to the printed totals settled it. The 2022 report prints a 2021 total, and the category rows sum to every printed total within one kwacha. The 2021 bar is back, and a check now compares the category rows with the printed totals on every run.
  • Errors in my own table. Reading the case-story pages showed a contract listing two of its four contractors, and a row citing the wrong page. Both corrected.
  • A claim the site could not back. The site said "every figure is page-cited". Contract and supplier rows are; budget and findings figures cite the report year. The claim now says exactly that.

Data quality decisions

  • No estimates. Where a report does not state a value, the cell is empty and the reason is noted.
  • Currencies kept apart. Kwacha and US dollar amounts are never added together.
  • One outlier shown on its own. Unpaid tax arrears are a stock built up over years and dominate every findings total, so they are reported separately.
  • Unnamed suppliers stay unnamed. "Contractor (not named)" is recorded as such, never guessed.
  • PDF page, not printed page. The two differ, and only the PDF page lets someone jump straight to the source.
  • A finding is a question. Being named in an audit paragraph is not being found at fault, and the site says so wherever names appear.

What the data shows

  1. Budget execution rose but not steadily. Payments reached 76.3% of the final budget in 2022 and 86.3% in 2025, with 87.7% and 81.8% in between. The surplus of receipts over payments fell from K30.2 billion to K2.1 billion.
  2. Clean accounts, persistent problems. The consolidated accounts received an unqualified opinion every year from 2022 to 2025, while 26 of 39 finding categories recurred in every comparable year (2021 to 2023). Unpaid bills rose from K3.9 billion to K5.2 billion over that period.
  3. Delivery is the weak point in the cases the auditors chose to highlight. Of 34 contracts checked against their pages, 20 were not fully delivered at the audit cut-off. These are highlighted cases, not a random sample, so they show where problems were found, not how common they are.

What it cannot show

  • The data records each report's position on its audit date, so later deliveries and recoveries are not reflected.
  • Ministries' responses in later reports and before parliamentary committees are not included.
  • 2021 has no comparable budget statement, and comparable findings totals exist only for 2021 to 2023; the 2024 and 2025 reports print none.
  • The organisation index captures about 94% of named companies, not all of them.

Reproduce it

Download the five reports from the Auditor General's website (ago.gov.zm), point the AG_PDF_DIR environment variable at that folder, clone the repository, run the R scripts in R/ in numbered order, and render the Quarto report. The data dictionary, methods note, sampling QA and coverage audit and changelog are in the repository. Errors can be reported through a form on the dashboard.
